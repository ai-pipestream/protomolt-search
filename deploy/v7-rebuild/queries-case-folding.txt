# Query set for the fold-then-stem A/B (SOURCE_STEMS vs
# SOURCE_NORMALIZED_STEMS on a body column).
#
# Chosen for the defect under test, not for general coverage. Porter only
# transforms lower-case input, so under SOURCE_STEMS a capitalized word is
# indexed as its literal surface form and never meets the stem its
# lower-case twin produces. That splits a term in two exactly where legal
# text capitalizes: captions, party names, court names, statutory titles,
# and the ALL-CAPS headings that opinions are full of.
#
# So these are lower-case queries whose target text is very often NOT.
# A query whose terms are lower-case in both the query and the corpus
# cannot distinguish the two chains, and padding the set with those would
# dilute the measurement into "no change" no matter what the truth is.
#
# Run:
#   ab --coord=... --queries=queries-case-folding.txt \
#      --arm=stems=body --arm=folded=body_norm --k=10

# Court and caption vocabulary: capitalized in nearly every opinion.

commissioner of internal revenue
national labor relations board

# Stemming and folding interacting: plurals and inflections of words that
# also appear capitalized. This is where the two chains should differ most,
# because SOURCE_STEMS folds neither and NORMALIZED_STEMS does both.
appellants argued the courts erred
defendants motions were denied
the judges findings of fact
plaintiffs claims were dismissed
respondents brief in opposition
petitioners application for review

# Control group: entirely lower-case in ordinary prose, so both chains
# should agree. Divergence HERE would mean the normalizer changed
# something other than case, which is the failure mode worth catching.
the burden of proof rests with the moving party
evidence was insufficient to support the verdict
remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion
the contract was unenforceable for lack of consideration
